Too many tabs? Let the butler tidy up.

Tab Butler is a free Chrome extension that sorts your tabs into colour groups automatically, puts idle tabs to sleep so Chrome stops eating your memory, and shows where your browsing time goes.

Everything your browser should do already

One click, or fully automatic. You choose.

🗂️

Auto tab groups

New tabs drop into the right colour group: Mail, Work, Dev, AI, Jobs, Social, Shopping and more. Your own groups are never touched.

😴

Sleep idle tabs

Tabs you haven't looked at for 30 minutes go to sleep and free up memory. They reload when you click them. Gmail, Meet and music stay awake.

🧹

Close duplicates

The same page open three times? One click keeps one. Optionally stop duplicates from opening at all.

💾

Sessions

Save a whole window as "Job hunt" or "Client work", close it, and bring it back later with the same groups.

⭐

Bookmark tidy-up

Builds a neat copy of your bookmarks sorted into folders and finds duplicates. Your originals are never moved or deleted, and a backup is one click.

📊

Browsing dashboard

See time per category and per site for the week, plus your most visited sites. Clean junk history (login pages, redirects, OTP pages) in one go.

Make it yours

Rename categories, change colours, add your own sites (your company tools, your college portal) and reorder groups. Export your settings and share them with your team.

Keyboard shortcuts: Alt+Shift+G sort tabs · Alt+Shift+D close duplicates · Alt+Shift+S sleep tabs.

Install in 2 minutes

Tab Butler is not in the Chrome Web Store yet, so you add it yourself. It's safe and takes four steps.

Download tab-butler.zip and double-click it to unzip. You get a folder called tab-butler. Keep it somewhere permanent, like Documents.
In Chrome, open chrome://extensions (copy it into the address bar).
Turn on Developer mode (switch at the top right).
Click Load unpacked and choose the tab-butler folder. Done! Click the puzzle icon 🧩 and pin Tab Butler.

Private by design

Tab Butler has no server, no account, no analytics and no ads. Your tabs, history, bookmarks and time stats are stored only in your own Chrome and never uploaded. It never reads page content, passwords or form data. The code is open, so anyone can check.

Why it asks for "history"

To show most-visited sites and to delete junk pages when you ask it to.

Why it asks for "bookmarks"

To count duplicates and create the sorted copy. It never deletes bookmarks.

Why it asks for "tabs"

To read tab addresses so it can group, sleep and de-duplicate them.

Questions

Will it close my tabs or lose my work?

No. Sorting only moves tabs into groups. Sleeping keeps the tab in place; it reloads when you click it (like Chrome's own Memory Saver). Tabs are only closed when you click "Close duplicates" or "Close all" yourself.

Does it work with many Chrome profiles?

Yes. Install it in each profile you want. Each profile keeps its own settings; use Export / Import settings to copy them across.

I already use tab groups. Will it mess them up?

No. Tab Butler only manages groups named after its own categories. Any group you create yourself is left exactly as it is.

Chrome shows "Disable developer mode extensions". Is that a problem?

That's Chrome's standard notice for extensions installed outside the Web Store. Click the X to dismiss it; Tab Butler keeps working.

How do I remove it?

Go to chrome://extensions and click Remove on Tab Butler. Your tabs, bookmarks and history stay as they are.
